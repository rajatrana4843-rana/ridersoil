What Is Cutting Oil and Why Does It Matter?

Cutting oil (also called metalworking fluid or coolant) is used during machining processes like turning, drilling, milling, grinding, and tapping. Its job is simple but critical:

  1. Reduce friction between the cutting tool and the workpiece
  2. Carry away heat generated during high-speed machining
  3. Prevent rust and corrosion on both the tool and the metal surface
  4. Flush away metal chips and fine particles
  5. Improve surface finish and extend tool life

Without a properly formulated cutting oil, tools wear out faster, machining accuracy drops, and production costs go up. This is exactly why the reputation and formulation expertise of your cutting oil manufacturer matters so much.

Types of Cutting Oil You Should Know About

Not every cutting oil is the same. A good cutting oil manufacturer usually offers a range of products to match different machining needs:

Neat (Straight) Cutting Oils –

Used without dilution, ideal for heavy-duty operations like gear cutting, broaching, and deep-hole drilling.

Soluble (Water-Miscible) Cutting Oils –

Mixed with water to form an emulsion, offering excellent cooling for high-speed operations.

Semi-Synthetic Cutting Oils –

A balanced blend of mineral oil and synthetic additives, giving good lubrication along with strong cooling.

Synthetic Cutting Oils –

Fully synthetic, oil-free formulations, known for excellent cooling, cleanliness, and long sump life.

A reliable cutting oil manufacturer should be able to guide you toward the right type based on your material (ferrous or non-ferrous), machine type, and operation.
