What Is Cutting Oil and Why It Matters

Cutting oil, also called cutting fluid, is used during machining operations like turning, drilling, milling, and grinding. When a cutting tool works on metal at high speed, it creates heat and friction. Without the right lubrication, this heat can damage the tool, ruin the surface finish, and slow down production. A good cutting oil reduces friction, removes heat, improves surface finish, and protects the tool and workpiece from rust — which is exactly why choosing the right cutting oil manufacturer directly affects your production quality and cost.

Maintenance and Best Practices

Even the best cutting oil will not perform well if it isn't maintained properly. For soluble oils, maintain the correct oil-to-water ratio, since this keeps cooling and lubrication balanced. Keep the coolant sump clean and free from metal chips, and check pH levels regularly, as a stable pH prevents rust and bacterial growth. Watch for tramp oil building up on the coolant surface, store drums in a cool, dry place away from sunlight, and top up small quantities regularly instead of letting the sump run dry. Replace the oil at the recommended interval rather than stretching its use, since old oil loses its cooling and protective properties.
